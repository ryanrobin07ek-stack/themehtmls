How to Measure Your Foot at Home

  1. Tape a piece of blank paper firmly to a hard floor against a straight wall.
  2. Stand straight with your heel flush against the wall wearing your typical socks.
  3. Mark the longest tip of your foot (usually the big toe or second toe) with a pencil.
  4. Measure the distance from the edge of the paper to the marked line in centimeters and refer to the table above.

Biomechanical Fit Guide

Understand your arch profile, pronation tendencies, and toe-box volume requirements.

High Arch (Supination)

Under-pronators require maximum plush cushioning to attenuate shock, as the foot does not naturally roll inward to absorb impact. Recommend: Cloud Nine Ultra or Velocity Aero Foam.

Neutral Arch

Efficient natural biomechanical landing. Standard neutral cushioning with balanced flexibility provides the ideal ride. Recommend: Alpha X2 or Nike Air Pulse.

Flat Foot (Overpronation)

Arches collapse inward under load. Requires structured medial guidance and firm dual-density heel support. Recommend: Nimbus Wide Fit (4E).

FEATURE ESSAY

The Engineering Behind Ultra-Rebound Foams

Published October 2026 • By Senior Footwear Scientist Dr. Aris Thorne

For over four decades, ethylene-vinyl acetate (EVA) dominated footwear midsoles. While durable, standard EVA absorbs impact energy by converting it into heat, returning less than 50% of the mechanical energy to the runner.

The introduction of supercritical fluid foaming using pressurized nitrogen gas created microcellular structures with closed gas pockets. This innovation raised energy rebound metrics past 85%, fundamentally altering the physics of distance running.

"Footwear is no longer simply protection against abrasions; it has become an external musculoskeletal propulsion system."

In our Stepz Pro series, this technology works in concert with carbon stabilization plates to reduce metabolic cost across 42km marathons by 4.2%.
